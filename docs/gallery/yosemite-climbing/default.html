Reading the Grades

A Yosemite grade is really three numbers stacked together: how long the day is, how hard the hardest free moves are, and how difficult the aid is.

Commitment Grade (I to VI)

  • I-II A few hours.
  • III Half a day.
  • IV A full day of technical climbing.
  • V A long day; many parties bivy once.
  • VI Multi-day wall for most teams.

Yosemite Decimal System

  • 5.0-5.7 Moderate; big holds, comfortable for new leaders.
  • 5.8-5.9 Solid intermediate. Valley 5.9 is famously stiff.
  • 5.10a-d Requires real crack technique.
  • 5.11+ Advanced; sustained jamming and stemming.
  • 5.12-5.14 Elite free climbing.

Aid Ratings

  • A0 Pulling on a bolt or pendulum; no real aid gear.
  • C1 Easy, bomber clean aid.
  • C2 Harder placements, still safe falls.
  • C3+ Delicate placements, serious falls.
  • The C means "clean," without a hammer. A once meant pitons.

Seriousness Suffixes

  • R Runout; a fall could mean injury.
  • X A fall could be fatal.
  • OW Offwidth: too wide to jam, too narrow to chimney. Valley specialty.

Valley Beta

General notes that apply across nearly every route in this guide.

Seasons

  • Spring (Apr-May) and fall (Sep-Oct) are prime for Valley-floor walls.
  • Summer is brutally hot on sunny faces; head high to Tuolumne or pick shaded aspects.
  • Half Dome and Snake Dike are best late spring through fall, once snow clears.
  • Winter brings storms, ice fall, and short days.

Permits and Rules

  • Overnight wall climbs require a free Big Wall Permit from the park.
  • Pack out all human waste on walls; bring a poop tube.
  • Never leave fixed ropes or haul bags abandoned on the wall.
  • Check park closures for raptor nesting, which can affect some walls in spring.

Gear Mindset

  • Valley cracks eat cams. Doubles to 3" is the baseline; walls often want triples.
  • Learn to tape hands; granite is abrasive.
  • Wide pieces (#4-#6) matter more here than almost anywhere else.
  • Always check a current topo; fixed gear changes.

Hazards

  • Rockfall is real and frequent. Wear a helmet, even at the base.
  • Crowds on the Nose and Half Dome mean dropped gear and passing parties.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms hit Half Dome hard in summer.
  • Descents cause many accidents. Know yours before you start.
This guide is an overview for inspiration and planning, not a topo. Route details, fixed gear, and conditions change. Bring a current guidebook, check recent trip reports, and climb within your experience.
