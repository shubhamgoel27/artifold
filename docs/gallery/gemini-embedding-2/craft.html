A paper, explained · arXiv 2605.27295

A short gallery tour of one embedding space

Here, a cooking video sits beside its recipe.

Gemini Embedding 2 turns video, audio, images, text, and any mix of them into one list of 3,072 numbers, so things that mean the same thing land in the same place. Google built it out of Gemini itself, and on the paper’s retrieval suite it beats the specialists.

Madhuri Shanbhogue, Zhe Li, Shanfeng Zhang and 85 others, Gemini Embedding Team, Google · submitted May 26, 2026

1

Notice where it starts: inside Gemini

The model is Gemini, retrained to read instead of write. Attention runs in both directions, every token’s vector is averaged, and one linear layer squeezes the result to 3,072 numbers. Gemini handles the formats, so raw images, video and audio go in as they are, in any order, even mixed inside one query.

Not on the label: how large the model is, how fast it runs, or what a call costs. The paper gives none of the three.

2

Each match is pulled close; the rest of the batch is pushed away

Training is contrastive. Given a query, the model has to pick its true match out of every other example in the batch, often plus one deliberately tricky decoy (on the map, boiling eggs is the decoy for boiling ramen). It happens in three stages.

  1. Pre-fine-tuneLots of noisy pairs, very large batches.
  2. Fine-tuneCurated tasks, hard decoys, batch size tuned per task.
  3. SoupAverage the weights of several fine-tuning runs.

Why soup works, from the video ablation: training on Vatex and MSR‑VTT lifted Vatex by 10.3 points but cost YouCook2 0.6. Averaged 1:1 with the original weights, all three sets came out ahead (+5.5, +0.9, +7.6).

The authors admit the balance between modalities was touchy, set by trial and error over sampling rates and batch sizes.

3

Against three rivals, the widest gap opens on photo‑plus‑question queries

Each row compares Gemini Embedding 2 (filled dot) with whichever rival did best on that task (open dot): Voyage‑3.5‑multimodal, Amazon Nova MME, or Google’s older multimodalembedding@001.

Read with care: rival scores were measured through their public APIs unless the vendor self-reported.

4

Look at astronomy, where every other model stalls near 30

Zero-shot, image-to-text, in fields nobody tuned it for. The specialists are moody: SigLIP 2 Giant scores 81.2 on recipes and 8.4 on fine art, and TIPS runs the other way. Gemini Embedding 2 never drops below 64.

Gap in the record: Voyage was scored on the first three fields only; its recipe cells are blank in the paper.

5

Here, the voice note skips the transcript, and finds more

In this test a spoken question has to find its answer among text passages. Fed the raw audio, the model beats the very same model reading an ASR transcript. A transcript has to commit: “recognize speech” or “wreck a nice beach.” The embedding doesn’t, and it keeps tone and emphasis too.

Small print: the paper doesn’t name the ASR system used for the baseline.

6

Text didn’t pay for the pictures

Teaching a text model three new senses usually blunts it. Here the multimodal model edges out its own text-only predecessor on multilingual text and jumps eight points on code. Much of the code gain is Gemini-written training data: on three code tasks it lifts the average from 73.0 to 86.3.
