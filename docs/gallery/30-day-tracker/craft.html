Two workouts, taken in turn

You need a pair of adjustable dumbbells (or a rack) and a bench. Week 1 is two sets of 8 with a weight that feels too easy. From week 2 on it’s three sets, aiming for 8–12 reps.

How the weight goes up

  1. Day 1: pick a weight you could lift about 15 times, and stop at 8. Easy is the point; you’re learning the movement.
  2. Every set, write the reps you got in its box. The boxes are your progress report.
  3. All three sets reached 12 with clean form? Next time, add the smallest jump you have (usually 1–2.5 kg or 5 lb) and start again at 8.
  4. Missed a day? Do the next workout in line. Never do two in one day.

The other days

  • Warm-up, every lift day5 min brisk walk or bike, then one light set of 8 of the first exercise.
  • WalkEasy enough to talk. 20 min in weeks 1–2, 30 min after that.
  • Thursday stretch, 10 minHip flexor stretch 45 s a side, cat–cow × 10, child’s pose 60 s, open-book rotations × 8 a side.

Sore or hurting?

Muscle soreness for a day or two after weeks 1 and 2 is normal, and it fades. Sharp or joint pain ends the set. Drop the weight, and if it comes back, ask a doctor or physio before your next session.

Rep range (8–12), 2–3 lifting days a week, and adding load once you beat the target reps follow the American College of Sports Medicine’s 2009 position stand on progression models. ACSM’s 2026 update puts consistency first and asks for every major muscle group at least twice a week; alternating A and B covers both. Rest times are this plan’s choice for beginners.
